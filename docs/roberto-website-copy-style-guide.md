# Robert MacNaughton — Website Copy Style Guide

*Adapted from the Professional Writing Style Guide v2. For use when writing or editing copy on RobertMacNaughton.com.*

---

## The Strategic Context

This website is primarily a **credibility artifact for referred visitors** — not a conversion funnel. Most people arriving here have already been told "you should talk to Robert" by someone they trust. The copy's job is to:

1. Confirm the referrer's judgment (yes, this is the real deal)
2. Give the visitor a feel for what it's like to be in Robert's world
3. Make it effortless to take the next step (usually scheduling a conversation)

The copy does NOT need to:
- Convince someone who's never heard of Robert to hire him
- Explain what executive coaching is
- Build urgency or overcome objections
- Compete on SEO for broad coaching keywords

This means the voice can be **confident without selling, specific without proving, warm without performing**.

---

## Translating the Email Voice to Website Copy

### What carries over directly from the email voice:

- **Warmth-first, substance-fast.** Just as emails open with a brief genuine connection before getting to business, website sections should feel welcoming but get to the point. No long wind-ups.
- **Direct coaching voice.** The same authority and specificity that shows up in client emails. No hedging, no "we might be able to help you think about possibly..."
- **Concrete over abstract.** Just as coaching emails include sample language clients can use, website copy should use specific examples over vague claims.
- **Brevity with depth.** Short paragraphs, high information density per sentence.
- **Invitation, not instruction.** The website should feel like an open door, not a sales floor.

### What shifts for website context:

| Email Voice | Website Adaptation |
|---|---|
| "Hi [Name] --" opening warmth | Warmth embedded in tone throughout, not as a greeting |
| Responds to specific context | Must speak to patterns across clients without naming anyone |
| Coaching advice with examples | Descriptions of approach with enough specificity to feel real |
| 1:1 relational register | 1-to-many, but should still feel like Robert talking to *you* |
| "Very respectfully, Robert" close | Clear, low-friction CTA — not formal |
| Natural typos, informal register | Clean but not polished to a corporate shine — preserve some texture |

## Voice Rules for Website Copy

### 1. First Person, Present Tense, Active

Write as Robert speaking directly. Not "Robert MacNaughton is an executive coach who..." but "I work with founders and executives who..."

The third-person bio voice on the NeubergGore site and LinkedIn profiles ("Robert MacNaughton is an executive coach and facilitator with 25 years of experience who specializes in...") is the *opposite* of how this site should sound. That's institutional copy. This is personal.

**Exception:** A short third-person bio block is fine for press/referral purposes, but it should feel different from the rest of the site — clearly a "for your reference" section, not the main voice.

### 2. Specificity Over Credentials

Roberto's emails never lead with credentials. He leads with what he sees and what he can do about it. The website should do the same.

**Instead of:** "With over 20 years of experience and 5,000+ hours of coaching..."
**Write like:** "I've spent most of my career in rooms with founders who are scaling faster than their leadership skills. I help close that gap."

The numbers (20 years, 5,000 hours, 1,200+ executives) can appear — but as supporting evidence, not as the opening move. Put them in a sidebar, a quick-facts section, or a secondary page. The main copy should lead with insight, not resume.

### 3. Name the Client's Experience, Not the Service

Roberto's coaching emails validate the client's experience before offering advice. The website should do the same — describe what the visitor is likely feeling or facing, then describe how Robert works.

**Instead of:** "I offer 1:1 executive coaching, team facilitation, and leadership assessment."
**Write like:** "If you're an engineer who just became a manager of managers, or a founder who's great at product but struggling with the people side of scaling — that's where I live."

### 4. Show the Methodology Through Example, Not Explanation

Roberto's best coaching emails include concrete frameworks: the 3-step leadership assessment follow-up script, the "lead with questions" mantra, the "What's Going Well / Opportunities to Optimize" structure. The website should hint at this depth without turning into a methodology deck.

**Good approach:** Share one or two specific frames or questions that give visitors a taste of the coaching. Example: "One of the first questions I ask a new client: 'What's the conversation you've been avoiding?'"

**Bad approach:** Listing all methodologies, certifications, and frameworks in a comprehensive overview.

### 5. Let Testimonials Do the Selling

Roberto's emails never make the case for himself — the quality of the advice IS the case. On the website, client quotes should carry the credibility weight. Roberto's own copy should be warm and clear; testimonials should be where the "this guy changed my leadership" message lives.

### 6. Geography and Presence

Roberto's emails often include "Cheers from Boulder" or "I'll be around the office." The website should convey his groundedness in Boulder without making it a limitation. Mention the in-person possibility for local clients, the ease of video for everyone else.

## Sentence-Level Guidelines for Web Copy

### Length and Rhythm
- **Average sentence length: 10–18 words.** Shorter than even the email voice — web readers scan.
- **Mix short punches (4–8 words) with one medium sentence per paragraph.**
- **Maximum sentence length: 25 words.** If it's longer, break it.
- **Paragraph length: 1–3 sentences maximum.** Single-sentence paragraphs are fine and encouraged for emphasis.

### Vocabulary (carry forward from email guide)
**Use:** skillfully, potent, inquiry, calibration, integrate, resonance, tensions, leverage (noun)
**Avoid:** synergize, circle back, holistic, robust, foster, streamline, landscape, navigate, delve, nuanced, pivotal — and any word that could appear in a generic LinkedIn coaching post

### Punctuation
- **Prefer periods and commas.** Use em dashes sparingly (max 1 per section).
- **Ellipsis (...) is fine** — it's part of Roberto's natural voice and creates a thinking-pause feel.
- **No exclamation points in main copy.** Reserve for a testimonial quote or a single, genuinely enthusiastic moment. Never two in a row.

## AI-Tell Inoculation (Website-Specific)

All rules from the email style guide apply, plus these website-specific additions:

### No Hero's Journey Framing
- **AI pattern:** "Every founder reaches a moment when everything changes. That's where the real work begins."
- **Roberto's approach:** Skip the mythological framing. State what happens plainly: "You got promoted. Now you manage managers. The skills that got you here won't get you there."

### No "Imagine This" Openings
- **AI pattern:** "Imagine walking into your next board meeting with complete confidence..."
- **Roberto's approach:** Don't ask the reader to imagine. Describe reality they'll recognize: "Your board wants a plan. Your co-founder wants to pivot. Your VP of Eng just quit. Monday morning."

### No Numbered Value Props in Threes
- **AI pattern:** "Three pillars of transformational leadership: Clarity. Courage. Connection."
- **Roberto's approach:** If there are three things, they should be unequal in weight and described in prose, not presented as a branded triptych.

### No "I Help X Do Y So They Can Z" Formula
- **AI pattern:** "I help ambitious founders build high-performing teams so they can scale with confidence."
- **Roberto's approach:** This formula is everywhere. Avoid it entirely. Describe who you work with and what changes, but not in this X→Y→Z structure.

### No Aspirational Identity Language
- **AI pattern:** "Become the leader your team deserves." / "Step into your full potential."
- **Roberto's approach:** Roberto's emails never promise transformation through identity language. He talks about skills, practices, and specific situations. The website should do the same.

## Section-by-Section Voice Notes

### Homepage / Hero
- 1–2 sentences, max. Not a paragraph.
- Name the type of person who lands here and what they're dealing with.
- Example tone: "I coach founders and executives through the leadership challenges that don't have playbooks."

### About / Bio
- First person for the main narrative. Third person for a compact reference bio.
- Lead with what you do and why, not credentials.
- Credentials appear but don't lead.
- Include the Boulder grounding — it's part of the identity.
- Mention the Integral Center, NeubergGore, and Antelope Recovery as the story of your path, not as resume bullets.

### How I Work / Approach
- Describe the experience of working with you, not a methodology breakdown.
- One or two signature moves or questions — enough to feel real, not a full curriculum.
- "Most of my clients come through referrals" — say this. It signals the right things without selling.

### Testimonials
- Let these do the heavy lifting on credibility.
- Attributed first names and roles/companies where possible.
- Short (2–4 sentences) and specific — not generic praise.

### Contact / CTA
- Single, clear action: schedule a conversation.
- Tone: "If someone you trust sent you here, let's talk." Not "Book your complimentary discovery session today!"
- Calendly link, direct. No intake form unless truly needed.

## Quick-Reference: Website Copy Checklist

Before publishing any section in Roberto's voice:

- [ ] Written in first person, present tense, active voice
- [ ] Leads with client experience or insight, not credentials
- [ ] Average sentence length under 18 words
- [ ] Paragraphs are 1–3 sentences
- [ ] No "Not X, it's Y" reframes
- [ ] No "quietly," "gently," or aspirational identity language
- [ ] Maximum one em dash per section
- [ ] No stacked rhetorical questions
- [ ] No hero's journey framing or "Imagine this" openings
- [ ] No "I help X do Y so they can Z" formula
- [ ] No generic coaching vocabulary (robust, holistic, navigate, landscape, foster)
- [ ] No numbered value props in symmetrical threes
- [ ] No inspirational kicker endings
- [ ] CTA is direct and low-friction
- [ ] Reads like Robert talking, not a coaching website template
- [ ] Specific enough that you couldn't swap in another coach's name and have it still work
