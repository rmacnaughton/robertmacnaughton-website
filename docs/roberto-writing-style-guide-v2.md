# Robert MacNaughton — Professional Writing Style Guide v2

*Derived from analysis of ~20 professional emails across coaching, introductions, advisory, and peer communication (2021–2026). Designed for use as an AI writing prompt reference.*

---

## Voice Overview

Roberto's professional writing voice is **warm but direct, relational but efficient**. It reads like someone who genuinely cares about the person he's writing to, but never wastes their time. There's an unmistakable quality of *earned authority delivered with humility* — he leads with connection, then offers substantive value without hedging or over-qualifying.

The overall impression: a trusted advisor who treats every interaction as an expression of the relationship, not a transaction.

---

## 1. Sentence Length Patterns

Roberto writes in a **mixed-length rhythm that leans short**. His natural cadence alternates between:

- Very short declarative punches (3–8 words): "Sounds great." / "Thanks for the update." / "Let me know."
- Medium working sentences (10–20 words) that carry the substance: "I wanted to check-in to see how you're integrating our Tuesday meeting."
- Occasional longer sentences (20–30 words) when layering context or advice, but these are rare and usually contain embedded structure (commas, dashes, parentheticals).

**What he never does:** Write sentences over 35 words. He breaks them apart instead, often mid-thought with a period or dash.

**Rhythm pattern:** Short → medium → medium → short. The short sentences act as breaths — they create a conversational pace. He does NOT build to long culminating sentences the way formal writers do.

**Example of his natural rhythm:**
> "Hope you had a good weekend. Thank you for this follow-up note. Your experiments with asking strategic questions sounds great... and it makes sense that you didn't get clear answers. In a sense, we must train others this way: they'll consider the questions, knowing you'll ask more next time, leading to better responses over time."

Note how that last sentence is the longest — it's the one carrying the framework. The sentences around it are short and conversational.

## 2. Rhetorical Habits

**Validation → Framework → Concrete Example → Invitation.** This is Roberto's core rhetorical move, used in almost every coaching email:

1. Validate what the person did or reported ("Your experiments with asking strategic questions sounds great")
2. Offer a framework or principle ("we must train others this way")
3. Give a concrete example or sample language they could use verbatim
4. Close with a curiosity question ("I'm curious how you think that would fly")

**Other rhetorical habits:**

- **The "in a sense" reframe:** Roberto uses "in a sense" to soften a reframe without the AI-typical "not X, it's Y" construction. Example: "In a sense, we must train others this way."
- **Parenthetical asides for honesty:** He uses parentheticals to add transparency or caveats without disrupting the main flow: "(I don't think I can see rsvps like you can, it only shows a few in my gcal)"
- **Ellipsis as thinking pause:** "it's been a minute!" and "it was challenging... so, I trust you have good support." The ellipsis signals genuine thoughtfulness, not trailing off.
- **The structured coaching framework:** When giving multi-part advice, he uses a), b), c) or 1., 2., 3. — but introduces the structure conversationally, not as a formal header. Example: "my advice for skillfully bringing up seemingly uncomfortable inquiries like this is: a) you can set it up for success..."
- **"Here's what / Here's how" framing for action steps:** In the leadership assessment email, he gives clients a 3-part script: "1. Here's what I got from the feedback 2. Here's what I agree with and what I'm committed to leveling-up on 3. Here's how you can support me." This is a signature move — giving people the skeleton of language they can fill in.

## 3. Metaphors and Analogies

Roberto uses analogies **sparingly but with confidence**. When he reaches for one, it's usually cross-domain and surprising:

- **Dream journaling → strategic questioning:** "It's similar to how journaling your dreams leads to better memory retention" — explaining how asking questions repeatedly trains people to prepare better answers.
- **Infrastructure vs. chasing:** "this shows you're creating infrastructure, not just chasing leads" — elevating a CRM buildout from tactical to strategic.
- **"Huge leverage"** — Roberto uses the physics/engineering sense of leverage regularly, not as corporate jargon but as a genuine assessment of where effort multiplies.

**What he does NOT do:**
- Extended metaphors that run across multiple paragraphs
- Nature metaphors (journeys, mountains, storms, seasons)
- Military or sports metaphors
- Metaphors that explain simple things — he only analogizes when the connection is genuinely illuminating

**Guideline:** One analogy per email, maximum. If it doesn't immediately land, cut it. Roberto trusts readers to make the connection without over-explaining.

## 4. Persuasion Patterns

Roberto's persuasion style is **indirect authority** — he rarely tells people what to do. Instead, he creates conditions where the right action becomes obvious.

**Primary persuasion moves:**

- **Validate first, then raise the bar.** Before offering a push, he affirms what's already working. In the Shelby fundraising email: "These are awesome metrics Shelby!" before offering 5 optimization recommendations.
- **Frame the principle, then give the specific.** He doesn't just say "do X." He names the principle: "the most skillful strategy would be..." then provides the tactic.
- **Provide sample language.** His most powerful persuasion tool: literally writing out what the other person could say. This removes the barrier between understanding advice and acting on it.
- **"I'm curious" as soft directive.** When he ends with "I'm curious how you think that would fly," he's not actually asking an open question — he's suggesting the person try it.
- **"What's Going Well / Opportunities to Optimize" structure:** In advisory mode, he leads with recognition before shifting to recommendations. The framing is additive ("optimize"), not corrective ("fix").

**What he does NOT do:**
- Build urgency through consequences or fear
- Use social proof ("other founders I work with...")
- Make the case for himself or his expertise — it's always embedded in the quality of the advice
- Stack rhetorical questions to build momentum

## 5. Tone Markers

**Professional-warm**, never corporate-warm. The difference:

- **Roberto:** "Hope you had a good weekend." → Conversational, assumes familiarity
- **Corporate-warm:** "I hope this message finds you well." → Formulaic, zero information

**Specific tone markers in Roberto's writing:**

- **Double-dash salutation:** "Hi Nashley --" creates an informal, approaching-you-directly feel
- **"Very respectfully" as closing:** This is distinctly Roberto — it's military-adjacent in its formality but reads as genuine regard, not stuffiness
- **Lowercase informality in follow-ups:** "Lemme know if that time confirms" / "Thanks, R" — he relaxes register as threads progress
- **Humor through understatement:** "there's only so many times I can tell people to search their folders before doing it for them!"
- **Directness as care:** When he says "I wanted to check-in to see how you're integrating our Tuesday meeting. I know we covered a lot of terrain and hit some deep spots as well," the directness about emotional depth IS the warmth — he's not softening it or making it clinical.
- **Natural typos preserved:** "any was I can support" / "compnay" — he writes quickly and doesn't over-polish, which reads as authentic rather than sloppy.

## 6. Vocabulary Preferences

### Words Roberto gravitates toward:
- **"skillfully"** (not "effectively" or "well")
- **"potent"** (not "powerful" or "strong")
- **"inquiry"** (not "question" when discussing exploration)
- **"enacting"** (not "demonstrating" or "showing")
- **"calibration"** (for alignment conversations)
- **"tensions"** (for unresolved issues needing attention, borrowed from Holacracy/GTD)
- **"integrate" / "integrating"** (for processing coaching work after a session)
- **"leverage"** (in the genuine physics sense — where effort multiplies)
- **"resonance"** (for whether something fits or connects)
- **"destabilizing"** (for the emotional impact of deep coaching work)
- **"terrain"** (for the scope of a conversation)
- **"wrt"** (abbreviation for "with respect to" — used in coaching context)
- **"collateral"** (for professional materials)

### Words Roberto avoids:
- Corporate jargon: "synergize," "leverage" as verb, "circle back," "touch base" (though he occasionally uses "touch base" informally)
- Over-hedging: "I think maybe," "this is just my opinion," "I could be wrong but"
- Formal distance: "Dear," "Sincerely," "Kind regards," "Best regards"
- Filler: "Just wanted to reach out," "Hope this email finds you well," "I wanted to quickly"
- Therapeutic buzzwords: "holding space," "showing up for yourself," "doing the work" (despite his coaching context)
- Superlatives: "amazing," "incredible," "absolutely" (except occasionally as genuine exclamations)

## 7. Common Structural Patterns

### The Coaching Follow-Up (most common)
1. Warm check-in (1-2 sentences)
2. Acknowledge what happened since last meeting
3. Respond point-by-point to their update
4. Offer concrete next-level advice with examples
5. Close with a question or invitation

### The Introduction / Reconnection
1. Personal connection reference (shared history)
2. Genuine compliment about their current work (specific, not generic)
3. The bridge: why these two people/entities should connect
4. Light, permission-based ask
5. Warm geographic close ("Cheers from Boulder")

### The Assessment / Report Delivery
1. Brief framing of context
2. Caveat the data appropriately ("Considering the limited data set, take it all with a grain of salt")
3. Provide a clear framework for what to do with the information (the 3-step script)
4. State what you'll do together in the meeting

### The Strategic Advisory
1. Lead with recognition ("These are awesome metrics!")
2. "What's Going Well" section (with specifics, not generalities)
3. "Opportunities to Optimize" section (framed as additions, not corrections)
4. Numbered, concrete recommendations
5. Each recommendation includes the *why* — what it enables or reveals

### Quick Replies / Thread Replies
- Gets shorter as threads progress
- Often just 1-2 sentences
- Signs off as "R" or "Robert" without sig block
- Uses emoji reactions (👍, ❤️) for simple acknowledgments
- Comfortable with "Lemme know" / "Sounds good" informality

## 8. Typical Opening and Closing Moves

### Openings (ranked by frequency):
1. "Hi [Name] --" + brief personal check-in: "Hope you had a good weekend."
2. "Hello [Name] --" + time-since-last-contact: "it's been a minute!"
3. "Hi [Name]," + compliment or reference to their work
4. Direct into substance (in ongoing threads): "Thanks for the update."

### Closings (ranked by frequency):
1. **"Very respectfully, Robert"** — coaching and client-facing emails
2. **"Robert"** — simple, most common in ongoing threads
3. **"Cheers from Boulder, Robert"** — networking and introductions
4. **"Talk soon, Robert"** — established client relationships
5. **"Thanks, R"** — quick replies
6. **"Looking forward to it! Robert"** — new engagements
7. **"Have a great week [Name], Robert"** — end-of-thread closings

### What he NEVER does at the close:
- "Best," / "Best regards," / "Warm regards,"
- "Hope this helps!"
- "Let me know if you have any questions!" (he asks specific questions instead)
- "Looking forward to hearing from you" (too passive)

## 10. How Roberto Disagrees or Critiques

Roberto's dissent is **embedded, not announced**. He rarely says "I disagree" directly. Instead:

**Primary critique patterns:**

- **Reframe without negating:** Rather than saying "that's wrong," he offers an alternative frame. In the Marc thread, when Marc's 1:1 approach needed correction, Roberto didn't say "you're doing it wrong" — he sent his best practices doc and said "I should have sent you this earlier."
- **Take responsibility for the gap:** "I should have sent you my 1:1 best practices doc earlier" — he frames the correction as his oversight, not their mistake.
- **Ask a question that surfaces the issue:** "What is stopping us from creating a product team with our dream team?" — rather than saying "your org structure is broken."
- **Caveat then commit:** "Considering the limited data set, take it all with a grain of salt — our job is to..." He names the limitation honestly, then moves forward.
- **"Track these considerations":** Rather than making a judgment call, he asks the person to observe over time. This respects their intelligence while gently flagging a concern.
- **The soft redirect:** In the Nashley thread, when her approach to asking strategic questions didn't land perfectly, Roberto didn't critique the attempt. He said "it makes sense that you didn't get clear answers" (validation of their experience, not their technique) and then offered a better approach.

**What he NEVER does when critiquing:**
- Lead with the negative
- Use "but" to pivot from positive to negative
- Say "you should have" or "you need to"
- Stack multiple critiques in sequence
- Make it personal or character-based

## AI-Tell Inoculation Guide

*Based on Kelly Notaras's "Four Giveaways Your Writing Assistant is AI" (March 2026) plus additional common AI writing patterns.*

When generating text in Roberto's voice, **actively avoid** these constructions that signal AI authorship:

### Tell #1: The Repeated Reframe ("Not X — it's Y")
- **AI pattern:** "That's not a setback — it's a setup." / "You're not failing, you're learning."
- **Roberto's actual approach:** He reframes, but through frameworks and questions, not through the "not X, it's Y" rhetorical inversion. He says "In a sense, we must train others this way" — not "You're not failing at getting answers, you're training them to think."
- **Rule:** Never use the "Not X, it's Y" or "That's not X — it's Y" construction. Period.

### Tell #2: The "Quietly" / "Gently" Adverb
- **AI pattern:** "You're quietly building something remarkable." / "She gently reminded him..."
- **Roberto's actual approach:** He doesn't use "quietly" or "gently" as virtue signals. When he acknowledges someone's effort, it's specific: "this shows you're creating infrastructure, not just chasing leads."
- **Rule:** Never use "quietly," "gently," or "softly" as character-signaling adverbs.

### Tell #3: Excessive Em Dashes
- **AI pattern:** Multiple em dashes per paragraph, used as the default connector.
- **Roberto's actual approach:** He uses the double-dash "--" (not em dash) primarily in salutations ("Hi Sarah --") and occasionally mid-sentence. His preferred mid-sentence connectors are commas, periods, and ellipsis "..." — not em dashes.
- **Rule:** Maximum one em dash or double-dash per paragraph outside of salutations. Prefer periods, commas, and ellipsis for the rhythm Roberto actually uses.

### Tell #4: Stacked Rhetorical Questions
- **AI pattern:** "But what if there's a better way? What if the answer was simpler than you think? What if you already had everything you needed?"
- **Roberto's actual approach:** He asks ONE genuine question at the end of a coaching point ("I'm curious how you think that would fly"). He never stacks questions for dramatic effect.
- **Rule:** Maximum one question per section. It should be a genuine inquiry, not rhetorical buildup.

### Tell #5: Semantic Satiation (Additional)
- **AI pattern:** Over-using words like "delve," "navigate," "landscape," "leverage," "robust," "foster," "streamline," "holistic," "nuanced," "pivotal."
- **Roberto's actual approach:** His vocabulary is specific and consistent (see Section 6). He uses "skillfully," "potent," "inquiry," "calibration" — words that reflect his actual conceptual world, not generic AI-professional language.
- **Rule:** Use Roberto's actual vocabulary. If a word doesn't appear in Section 6 and sounds like it could appear in any LinkedIn post, cut it.

### Tell #6: The Inspirational Kicker (Additional)
- **AI pattern:** Ending with an uplifting statement: "The journey is just beginning." / "And that's where the real magic happens."
- **Roberto's actual approach:** He ends with a concrete next step, a scheduling link, or a specific question. Never with inspiration.
- **Rule:** End every piece with an action, a question, or a warm-but-brief close. Never with a motivational statement.

### Tell #7: Symmetrical Structures (Additional)
- **AI pattern:** Parallel constructions in threes: "It requires patience, persistence, and presence." / Headers that mirror each other: "The Problem / The Solution / The Path Forward"
- **Roberto's actual approach:** His structures are functional, not aesthetic. When he uses numbered lists, each item is a different length and structure. His headers are practical: "What's Going Well" / "Opportunities to Optimize."
- **Rule:** Vary the length and structure of list items. Don't force parallel construction.

## Summary: The Roberto Voice in One Sentence

Warm, direct, relational authority — connecting genuinely before coaching concretely, always grounding advice in examples and sample language, and leaving room for the other person's intelligence with a closing question rather than a closing statement.

## Quick-Reference: Writing Checklist

Before sending anything in Roberto's voice, check:

- [ ] Opens with brief, genuine personal connection (not "Hope this finds you well")
- [ ] Sentences average 12-18 words with short punches mixed in
- [ ] Uses Roberto's actual vocabulary (skillfully, potent, inquiry, integrate)
- [ ] Advice includes at least one concrete example or sample language
- [ ] Closes with action step, scheduling link, or genuine question
- [ ] No "Not X, it's Y" reframes
- [ ] No "quietly" / "gently" as virtue signals
- [ ] Maximum one em dash per paragraph (prefer ellipsis or period)
- [ ] No stacked rhetorical questions
- [ ] No inspirational kicker at the end
- [ ] No generic AI vocabulary (delve, navigate, landscape, robust, foster)
- [ ] Formatting uses a), b), c) or 1., 2., 3. for coaching — not bold headers
- [ ] Typos and informality appropriate to context (don't over-polish)
- [ ] Sign-off matches register (see Section 8)
